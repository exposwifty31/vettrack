# Redesigning the VetTrack Critical-Equipment Board for a Wall-Mounted TV
### Council report — Claude Fable 5

**Subject:** vettrack.uk/board — Hebrew RTL, dark-themed critical-equipment monitoring dashboard for a veterinary clinic, displayed on a wall-mounted TV (glanceable, no input devices). Screenshot reviewed: 10:18 state showing "17 הצעות ממתינות לאישור" banner, two zeroed counter bars (רשימת המתנה / היערכות), a large empty center with a small checkmark ("כל הציוד הקריטי זמין"), a right rail with an empty-state circle ("אין ציוד קריטי מוגדר"), an אחראים sign-in list (all "לא סומן"), and small חשמל / עמדות עגינה cards.

---

## 0. The framing insight: this board's states are inverted

Before any styling recommendation, the screenshot reveals a structural problem worth naming: **the board's visual weight is inversely proportional to information value.** The one genuinely actionable item — 17 proposals awaiting approval — is rendered as a thin, low-contrast gray strip. The "everything is fine" state — the single most common state the board will ever display — is a tiny checkmark floating in a sea of dead black pixels, occupying perhaps 2% of screen area. Meanwhile two counter bars that read "0" consume two full-width rows. Wallboard design guidance is explicit that every widget must earn its place by changing a viewer's next action, and that teams should "rehearse failure modes — preview empty and stale states before you trust the wall" ([Dataplicity wallboard guide](https://docs.dataplicity.com/workflows/design-the-wallboard-for-who-walks-past-it)). This board was clearly designed around its populated state and its empty state was never treated as a first-class design target — yet in a well-run clinic, the empty state *is* the default state, possibly 95% of operating hours.

The redesign should therefore be conceived as a small **state machine with dramatically different full-screen presentations**, not a fixed grid that happens to be empty sometimes. I'd define four global states: (1) **All clear** — celebratory, calm, full-screen; (2) **Attention** — items waiting (approvals, sign-ins missing); (3) **Alert** — critical equipment down or power alerts > 0; (4) **Unconfigured/stale** — no equipment defined, or data feed dead. Each state should be recognizable *from the corridor, peripherally, without reading* — by overall screen color temperature and dominant shape. This is the core promise of calm/ambient display theory: information "easily moves from centre to periphery and back," using "subtle changes to reflect updates without distracting" ([Weiser & Brown's calm technology criteria, via Newcastle University thesis](https://theses.ncl.ac.uk/jspui/bitstream/10443/5438/1/Ku%C4%8Dera%20Jan%20e-copy.pdf); [Calm Tech Institute principles](https://www.calmtech.institute/calm-tech-principles)). A nurse should know from 8 meters, mid-stride, whether the screen is "green," "amber," or "red" overall — today the screen communicates nothing at that distance.

One more zero-cost, high-impact observation from the screenshot itself: **the board is running inside a full Chrome window with ~14 tabs, a bookmarks bar, and the URL bar visible on the TV.** That chrome consumes roughly 12% of vertical resolution and adds permanent visual noise (and permanent static bright elements — a burn-in risk, see §3). Before any redesign ships, the display should run in kiosk/fullscreen mode on a dedicated non-admin account; Klipfolio's TV-dashboard guidance lists a dedicated machine, non-admin account, and full-screen mode as the very first steps for wall displays ([Klipfolio](https://www.klipfolio.com/resources/articles/best-practices-large-screen-wallboard-tv-dashboard)).

## 1. Typography for 10-foot viewing

The current type is web-scale, not TV-scale. Labels like "לא סומן" and the counter captions appear to be in the 12–16 px range at 1080p — well below every published TV floor. The consensus across platforms: Apple's tvOS sets **body at 29 pt with a 23 pt minimum** ([Apple HIG Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography)); Amazon Fire TV requires body text of at least 14 sp ≈ **28 px at 1080p** ([Amazon Fire TV guidelines](https://developer.amazon.com/docs/fire-tv/design-and-user-experience-guidelines.html)); practitioner syntheses recommend **34–36 px body / 28–30 px secondary as the absolute floor at 1080p, with nothing anywhere below 28 px** — including timestamps and footers ([UI/UX Atlas 10-foot UI lesson](https://uiuxatlas.com/lessons/responsive/tv-and-10-foot-ui-design/)). Google's TV guidance simply says to "prioritize larger typography" and avoid thin weights whose strokes "are not instantly recognisable" at distance ([Android TV Typography](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/tv/guides/styles/typography)).

But those platform numbers assume a couch at 3 m. A corridor-mounted clinic board is often read from 4–6 m while walking, which pushes into **digital-signage** territory, where the governing rule is physical letter height, not pixels: roughly **1 inch (≈72 pt) of letter height per 10 feet of viewing distance** for reading text, and 1 inch per 3–4 feet for headline/glance content ([TVpilot signage font guide](https://tvpilot.app/blog/digital-signage-font-size-guide); [48HourPrint/JMU rule](https://www.48hourprint.com/banner-font-size.html)). One signage reference tabulates: at 10 ft, 36 pt minimum body and 72 pt headlines; at 15 ft, 48 pt body and 96 pt headlines ([digitalsignage.com typography guide](https://digitalsignage.com/digital_signage/docs/guides/typography-viewing-distance/)). Translating to a concrete scale for a 55" 1080p panel viewed at ~4 m, I'd specify a **five-step scale** (Smashing Magazine recommends starting TV design with a small 5–6 step scale — [Designing for TV, Part 2](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2025/09/designing-tv-principles-patterns-practical-guidance/)):

| Token | Size @1080p | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `display` | 120–160 px, bold | Hero numbers (17 approvals; count of equipment down) |
| `headline` | 64 px | Global state line ("כל הציוד הקריטי זמין") |
| `title` | 40 px | Card titles (חשמל, אחראים, עמדות עגינה) |
| `body` | 32 px | Row content, staff names |
| `caption` | 28 px, never smaller | Timestamps, footer — used sparingly |

Weight and rendering matter as much as size. TVs sit farther, and panels apply sharpening and sub-sampling, so **avoid weights below 400; prefer 500–700 for anything that carries a number**, avoid hairline borders, and keep line lengths under ~60 characters ([UI/UX Atlas](https://uiuxatlas.com/lessons/responsive/tv-and-10-foot-ui-design/); [tvOS distance rules — "no thin fonts or hairline strokes"](https://skills.sh/ehmo/platform-design-skills/tvos-design-guidelines)). Marvel's TV design team put it bluntly: "When in doubt, go larger. Bump up both the size and weight of your type," and — critically for a one-off installation like this — **test by physically standing where staff stand** ([Marvel, Designing for Television](https://marvelapp.com/blog/designing-for-television/)). The single most effective acceptance test for this redesign costs nothing: stand at the far end of the corridor and check that the global state and the hero number are readable in a 2–3 second glance ([digitalsignagehub font guide](https://digitalsignagehub.org/digital-signage-font-size-guide/)).

A Hebrew-specific amplifier: Hebrew has no capital letters and its letterforms are differentiated largely in fine interior details (ד/ר, ה/ח, ו/ז), so it needs *more* size headroom than Latin at the same distance, not the same. RTL localization guidance consistently recommends setting Hebrew/Arabic 1–3 px larger than the equivalent Latin size and increasing line-height (≈1.5–1.7) ([JetRuby RTL design guide](https://jetruby.com/blog/creating-an-impressive-right-to-left-design/); [Hebrew RTL best-practices skill](https://claudemarketplaces.com/skills/skills-il/localization/hebrew-rtl-best-practices)). Treat my table above as Latin-equivalent minimums and nudge Hebrew up one notch.

## 2. Color and contrast for TV panels

The current palette is the classic "dark theme that's actually invisible": near-black cards (#1a1a1a-ish) on pure black, with mid-gray labels. On a TV this fails twice. First, contrast: TV panels vary wildly in calibration, are often in "Vivid"/"Eco" modes, and fight ambient clinic lighting; 10-foot guidance therefore targets **7:1 (WCAG AAA) for text**, not the web's 4.5:1 ([UI/UX Atlas](https://uiuxatlas.com/lessons/responsive/tv-and-10-foot-ui-design/); [PowerBI Wall's TV dashboard spec](https://powerbiwall.com/how-to-design-dashboards-for-tv-displays)). The mid-gray "לא סומן" labels and the gray banner text almost certainly land in the 2–3:1 range against their card backgrounds — decorative at 10 feet. A practical rule from wallboard practice: "stop using mid-gray for anything that carries a number" ([danceparty.ai office-TV rules](https://danceparty.ai/articles/office-tv-dashboard-design-rules)).

Second, surface strategy. Keep the dark theme — light-on-dark is the correct default for TV ("Use light text on a dark background. This style is easier to read on a TV" — [Android TV style guidance](https://spot.pcc.edu/~mgoodman/developer.android.com/preview/tv/design/style.html), and tvOS itself uses a dark theme with 4.5:1+ contrast — [tvOS guidelines](https://skills.sh/ehmo/platform-design-skills/tvos-design-guidelines)) — but rebuild it on **dark gray, not pure black**. Material Design's dark theme uses **#121212** as the base precisely because gray surfaces can express elevation and depth, whereas cards on pure black collapse into the background ([Material Design dark theme](https://m2.material.io/design/color/dark-theme.html)). Build 3 elevation steps (base ~#121212 → cards ~#1E1E24 → focal/hero surface ~#26262E), with "the higher the surface, the lighter the gray" ([elevation guide](https://medium.muz.li/mastering-elevation-for-dark-ui-a-comprehensive-guide-04cc770dd0d6)). Use off-white (#E8E8EA) rather than pure white for body text to avoid harsh halation on high-brightness panels ([DesignStudio dark-mode practices](https://www.designstudiouiux.com/blog/10-dark-mode-ui-best-practices/)). A subtle blue-tinted gray reads more "medical/professional" than neutral black and is a common recommendation for dark palettes ([Toptal dark UI principles](https://www.toptal.com/designers/ui/dark-ui-design)).

Status color semantics need discipline and redundancy. The board already uses green (✓, "2 מחובר") and red ("0 התראות חשמל" — oddly rendered in red even when the value is zero, which cries wolf). Rules: (a) **color-code the state, not the label** — a red "0" is semantically backwards; zero alerts should be green or neutral, and red should be reserved exclusively for active problems, consistent with the standard vocabulary green=normal, amber=degraded, red=failed, gray=unknown ([colorfyi dashboard color reference](https://colorfyi.com/blog/color-in-dashboard-design/)); (b) since ~8% of men have red-green color vision deficiency, **never encode status in hue alone** — pair every status with an icon shape (✓ / ⚠ / ✕) and a text label, and prefer a reddish-orange/bluish-green pair over pure red/green ([MDN, Use of color](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Accessibility/Guides/Understanding_WCAG/Perceivable/Use_of_color); [healthcare palette guidance — "every status indicator in a healthcare interface should have a non-color encoding"](https://colorarchive.org/guides/color-palette-for-healthcare/)); (c) each status chip should hit at least 3:1 against its card ([accessible dashboard combinations](https://colorpalapp.vercel.app/guides/accessible-dashboard-color-combinations)).

Finally, a TV-physics note that also shapes color: this appears to be a consumer TV running static content ~10 hours/day. Consumer panels — especially OLED — develop image retention/burn-in from static logos, bars, and bright fixed elements; commercial signage displays mitigate this, consumer sets don't ([Samsung consumer-vs-commercial comparison](https://image-us.samsung.com/SamsungUS/home/resources/consumer-tvs-vs-commercial-displays/INFOGRAPHIC_Samsung_Seeing-the-Big-Picture_Commercial-Displays-or-Cons.pdf); [signage glossary on retention](https://cast-hub.com/digital-signage-glossary/)). Design mitigations: dark theme (already helps — it minimizes total pixel activation), avoid large fields of saturated static color, keep brightness ≤60%, and see §5 for motion-based mitigation. Pixel-shift on the TV helps edges but "does very little for large static elements" ([OLED Guard analysis](https://oledguard.com/pixel-shift)), so the design itself must avoid never-changing bright blocks.

## 3. Layout and information hierarchy

The current layout is a web app's sidebar-and-content pattern ported to a TV, which produces its worst pathology: the visually dominant area (the huge center) carries the least information density, while dense, small content (staff list, power cards) is crammed into a narrow right rail at caption sizes. For a glanceable board, layout should follow a strict altitude model: **one global answer, 3–5 supporting numbers, and nothing else.** Wall-display practice converges on 5–7 KPIs max ([Hart IT](https://hartit.io/displaying-your-dashboard-on-a-tv-screen)), ≤7 visualization cards per screen ([PowerBI Wall](https://powerbiwall.com/how-to-design-dashboards-for-tv-displays)), and the test that "every component should be answerable in a two-to-three-second glance" ([IGC operational dashboards](https://www.intelligentgraphicandcode.com/design/dashboard-design/operational-dashboards)). This board currently has ~7 regions but only ~3 carry meaning at any given time.

Concretely, I'd restructure into a **three-band RTL layout** (reading starts top-right in Hebrew — [Material 3 bidirectionality](https://m3.material.io/foundations/layout/bidirectionality-rtl)):

1. **Status band (top ~15%):** clock + department name at top-right (kept), and — replacing the anemic gray banner — a full-width **state strip** whose background tint encodes the global state. "17 הצעות ממתינות לאישור" becomes a hero number (120 px) with a short label, positioned right. This is the board's single most actionable datum and should be its loudest element.
2. **Main stage (middle ~60%):** in the *alert* state, large equipment cards (one per down/degraded device: name, location, status icon, minutes elapsed). In the *all-clear* state, this becomes the celebratory full-stage state screen (§4) — not an empty grid. Counter bars for רשימת המתנה / היערכות should not exist as full-width rows when zero; collapse them into compact chips in the status band and let them expand into the main stage only when non-zero. Empty rows are rented space; "trim components that don't add real insight" ([Klipfolio](https://www.klipfolio.com/resources/articles/best-practices-large-screen-wallboard-tv-dashboard)).
3. **Ops band (bottom ~25%):** three equal cards — אחראים (sign-ins), חשמל, עמדות עגינה — at title/body sizes. The staff list should switch from a name-by-name "לא סומן" litany to an aggregate ("0/5 סומנו" with a segmented progress indicator plus the names), because five identical gray failure rows are unreadable at distance but a "0 of 5" fraction is instantly parseable. Note: per Material 3, a progress indicator in **Hebrew should retain LTR fill direction** — an interesting Hebrew-specific exception to RTL mirroring ([Material 3](https://m3.material.io/foundations/layout/bidirectionality-rtl)).

Two cross-cutting layout rules. First, **TV-safe margins**: TVs still overscan; keep all content inside ~95% of the frame — Apple specifies 60 pt top/bottom and 80 pt side insets at 1080p, Android TV a 5% margin ([platform safe-zone comparison](https://ux.detroit3d.com/platforms/tv-10-foot.html)). The current footer strip ("עודכן 10:18" bottom-left, "מרכז ניטור ציוד" bottom-right) sits at the very edge and risks being cropped on some panels. Second, **hierarchy through size ratio, not position alone**: make the one number that matters ~2× the size of every other number on screen ("Put six numbers on a wall screen and make one of them twice the size of the other five" — [danceparty.ai](https://danceparty.ai/articles/office-tv-dashboard-design-rules)). Since there's no cursor, everything must be visible by default — no hover states, no truncation-with-tooltip, no scroll ([IGC — "No hover states: there is no mouse cursor on a wall display"](https://www.intelligentgraphicandcode.com/design/dashboard-design/operational-dashboards)).

## 4. Empty states: the board's two empties are different problems

The screenshot actually contains **two semantically opposite empty states rendered almost identically, and they contradict each other.** The center says "כל הציוד הקריטי זמין" (success: everything monitored is fine) while the right rail says "אין ציוד קריטי מוגדר... לא סומן ציוד קריטי במרפאה זו" (configuration failure: nothing is being monitored at all). If no critical equipment is *defined*, the claim that all critical equipment is *available* is vacuously true and operationally misleading — staff may believe monitoring is active when it isn't. Empty-state doctrine distinguishes these cases sharply: a *completion/success* empty should celebrate ("All caught up") while a *first-run/unconfigured* empty must explain what's missing and how to fix it ([NN/g, Designing Empty States in Complex Applications](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/empty-state-interface-design/); [empty-state pattern taxonomy](https://usertourkit.com/blog/empty-states-that-convert-onboarding-design-patterns)). The board must pick one global truth: **if zero devices are configured, the whole board should enter the "unconfigured" state** — an amber, unmistakable screen saying "המערכת אינה מנטרת ציוד — יש להגדיר ציוד קריטי במערכת הניהול," naming where to fix it (on the management console, since the TV has no input). Never let a configuration hole masquerade as good news.

For the genuine all-clear state (once equipment is configured), the current tiny checkmark under-sells the system's most frequent message. Design it as a **confident full-stage composition**: a large (≈160–200 px) check emblem, the headline at 64 px, and — crucially — *evidence of life*: "12 פריטי ציוד תקינים · נבדק לאחרונה 10:18" plus a quiet strip of the monitored equipment as small green-checked tiles. This follows the status-page pattern where "All Systems Operational" is a prominent, positively-designed banner backed by a component list, not an absence ([PagerDuty status-page best practices](https://www.pagerduty.com/resources/outages/learn/status-page-best-practices/)). Showing the monitored inventory in the all-clear state also solves a trust problem specific to ambient displays: a blank screen is indistinguishable from a crashed screen. NN/g's first rule applies directly: "Do not default to totally empty states — users may be left wondering if the system is still loading or if errors have occurred" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/empty-state-interface-design/)).

The micro-empties deserve the same treatment. "לא סומן" repeated five times is an anti-pattern — the empty state should "replace the element that would ordinarily show" rather than render five hollow rows ([Carbon Design System empty-states pattern](https://carbondesignsystem.com/patterns/empty-states-pattern/)), hence the 0/5 aggregate proposed in §3. And copy should name the noun and the consequence in the product's vocabulary ("אף אחראי לא נרשם למשמרת הבוקר") rather than a bare status word — specific, honest labels beat generic ones ([72technologies empty-state analysis](https://www.72technologies.com/blog/empty-states-that-convert); [Eleken empty-state rules](https://www.eleken.co/blog-posts/empty-state-ux)). Because the display is non-interactive, every empty state's "call to action" must be *deictic* — pointing at where the action happens (which console, which app), not a button.

## 5. Motion, auto-refresh, and data trust

A wall display's motion budget is small but essential, and it serves three jobs: signaling liveness, directing attention to change, and (on this hardware) fighting burn-in. For liveness: the footer already shows "עודכן 10:18," which is the right instinct — every serious always-on dashboard surfaces a last-updated timestamp — but it's caption-sized in the burn-prone bottom corner. Promote it into the status band as an auto-ticking "עודכן לפני 40 שניות" with a **stale-data escalation**: neutral when fresh, amber when past a staleness threshold, and a full-screen "נתונים לא עדכניים" takeover if the feed dies ([Smashing Magazine, UX strategies for real-time dashboards — the "Data Freshness Indicator" pattern](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2025/09/ux-strategies-real-time-dashboards/); [MetricUI's live/stale badge with amber-after-N-minutes default](https://www.metricui.com/docs/dashboard-header)). For a critical-equipment board the failure mode of silent staleness is the most dangerous state the product has — worse than any red alert — and it must be designed, not defaulted. Calm-technology principle #6 applies: technology should "still work when it fails," i.e., degrade legibly ([Calm Tech Institute](https://www.calmtech.institute/calm-tech-principles)). A common wallboard trick that needs no reading at all: a small "heartbeat" dot that pulses once per successful poll — motion in the periphery is preattentively detected, so its *absence* is noticed too.

For change-signaling: when a value updates, cross-fade or briefly highlight only the changed element — "emphasize the data that changed and keep secondary elements calm" ([motion patterns for dashboards](https://themotiondot.com/blog/motion-design-data-dashboards-7-patterns)). Motion is the strongest preattentive attention magnet available ("Motion or transition captures attention and signals change, urgency, or activity" — [glanceable-interface principles](https://medium.com/design-bootcamp/design-for-glanceable-interfaces-how-preattentive-vision-shapes-intuitive-interactions-d2042b119280)), which cuts both ways: reserve sustained animation (a slow pulse) exclusively for the *alert* state, so that a pulsing red card in the corner of a nurse's eye is unambiguous. Everything else should change with ≤300 ms cross-fades; no scrolling tickers, no perpetual spinners, no decorative animation — ambient displays must "use subtle changes... without distracting the user from their primary task" ([IFIP ambient display design](https://dl.ifip.org/db/conf/interact/interact2011-2/OcchialiniEE11.pdf)).

Refresh cadence and burn-in interact nicely here. Poll every 30–60 s (Datadog-style wallboard guidance suggests 60–120 s and disabling all interactivity so the view can't drift — [webeyez Datadog TV-mode guide](https://webeyez.com/insights/guides/datadog-dashboard-tv-mode-guide)). If the clinic wants secondary content (waiting list, preparedness), **rotate views on a 30–60 s cycle** rather than densifying one screen ([IGC — auto-rotation between views](https://www.intelligentgraphicandcode.com/design/dashboard-design/operational-dashboards); [VuePilot — keep rotations short](https://www.vuepilot.com/blog/tv-dashboard-productivity-tips)). Rotation doubles as burn-in protection by moving bright elements — important because the panel's own pixel-shift "cannot save a panel from a logo or menu bar that sits in roughly the same place for thousands of hours" ([TuckBar pixel-shift analysis](https://tuckbar.com/blog/pixel-shifting-for-oled-burn-in-prevention/)). Add an application-level 1–2 px slow drift of the whole layout every few minutes, a night mode (dimmed or clock-only outside clinic hours via schedule), and keep TV-side protections on (pixel shift, logo dimming, brightness ~60%) ([OLED prevention settings roundup](https://smarttvs.org/oled-burn-in-prevention-settings-2026/); [ScreenTest burn-in guidance](https://screentest.io/burn-in-prevention)).

## 6. RTL and Hebrew specifics

The board's RTL fundamentals look right (title top-right, rail on the right, right-aligned labels), but several Hebrew-specific refinements separate "translated" from "native." **Typeface:** the current font renders Hebrew thinly; at TV distances this is fatal (see §1's note on Hebrew's interior-detail letterforms). Use a Hebrew-first sans with generous counters and multiple weights — the standard modern stack is **Heebo, Assistant, Rubik, or Noto Sans Hebrew** ([RTL testing guide's Hebrew web-font list](https://placeholdertext.org/blog/the-complete-guide-to-rtl-right-to-left-layout-testing-arabic-hebrew-more/); [Hebrew RTL best-practices reference](https://claudemarketplaces.com/skills/skills-il/localization/hebrew-rtl-best-practices)). Heebo is particularly apt here since it's a Hebrew-native extension of Roboto with solid weight coverage for the bold hero numerals. Typography rules that differ from Latin: **never add letter-spacing to Hebrew**, avoid italics entirely (Hebrew has no native italic; browsers synthesize an ugly slant), avoid synthetic bolding, and raise line-height to ~1.5–1.7 ([same reference](https://claudemarketplaces.com/skills/skills-il/localization/hebrew-rtl-best-practices); [RTL typography guidance against bold/italic overuse](https://medium.com/@ilyaroytberg/development-and-design-of-rtl-website-peculiarities-cd4c027469ff)).

**Bidi handling of numbers and mixed text** is where this board is most at risk, because it's number-dense. Digits in Hebrew always run LTR and are never mirrored ([Apple HIG, Right to left — "the digits in a specific number always appear in the same order"](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/right-to-left); [Material bidirectionality](https://m2.material.io/design/usability/bidirectionality.html)); Hebrew users expect Western digits ([Workday Canvas RTL guidance](https://canvas.workday.com/guidelines/globalization/rtl-and-bidi)). The staff list mixes Latin ("ICU") with Hebrew ("בכיר ICU") — such embedded LTR runs must be isolated (`unicode-bidi: isolate` / `<bdo dir="ltr">`) or punctuation and word order will scramble; the same applies to the "תוספות / 0" composite in the docking-stations card, where the slash-number combination is a classic bidi trap ([Microsoft bidirectional text guidance on delimiter reordering](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/fin-ops-core/dev-itpro/user-interface/bidirectional-support); [Hebrew RTL practices on wrapping fixed-order values](https://lobehub.com/tr/skills/skills-il-localization-hebrew-rtl-best-practices)). Implementation should use `<html lang="he" dir="rtl">` plus CSS logical properties (`margin-inline-start`, `text-align: start`) throughout so cards mirror correctly without per-element hacks ([same source](https://claudemarketplaces.com/skills/skills-il/localization/hebrew-rtl-best-practices)).

**What not to mirror** deserves explicit listing for the redesign spec, because Hebrew has quirks even within RTL: checkmarks are never mirrored ([Adobe Spectrum bi-directionality](https://spectrum.adobe.com/page/bi-directionality/)); clocks and numeric time are not mirrored ([Material](https://m2.material.io/design/usability/bidirectionality.html)); and — counterintuitively — **Hebrew progress indicators and timelines should stay LTR**, unlike Arabic ([Material 3 — "Linear progress indicators should move from right to left for most RTL languages, except Hebrew where it should remain LTR"](https://m3.material.io/foundations/layout/bidirectionality-rtl)). So the proposed 0/5 sign-in progress bar and any "uptime last 24h" strip should fill left-to-right even on this fully RTL board. Academic work on bidi UIs confirms Hebrew numeric ranges and progressions tolerate LTR direction where generic RTL guidelines would say otherwise ([Towards the Right Direction in BiDirectional User Interfaces, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.08070)). Finally, keep visual hierarchy scanning from the **top-right**: the hero approval number belongs top-right, the state emblem centered, and the least important ops cards bottom-left — the mirror of the Z-pattern recommended for LTR TV dashboards ([PowerBI Wall Z-pattern](https://powerbiwall.com/how-to-design-dashboards-for-tv-displays), mirrored per [Material 3 RTL reading flow](https://m3.material.io/foundations/layout/bidirectionality-rtl)).

## 7. The redesigned layout, described

Pulling it together, here is the target design in one pass. **Chrome-free kiosk canvas, 1920×1080, content inside a 5% safe margin** ([safe zones](https://ux.detroit3d.com/platforms/tv-10-foot.html)). Base surface #121212 with a cool tint; cards #1E1E24 with 24 px radii and no hairline borders; off-white text; Heebo. The top band (≈150 px) holds, right-to-left: the clinic/department wordmark and clock (48–64 px, since a wall clock is a genuine utility for shift staff), a live freshness chip ("עודכן לפני 40 שנ׳" + heartbeat dot), and compact chips for רשימת המתנה and היערכות that stay collapsed at 0. Directly beneath, a full-width **state strip** whose fill tint (desaturated green / amber / red at ~15% opacity over the base) telegraphs global state to the periphery; on it, the hero item — today, "17" at ~140 px bold with "הצעות ממתינות לאישור" at 40 px and a note of where to approve them, since the TV itself accepts no input.

The main stage (≈600 px tall) is state-dependent. All-clear: centered 180 px check emblem in accessible green (#2E9E6B-family, paired with the ✓ shape for CVD redundancy — [colorfyi status vocabulary](https://colorfyi.com/blog/color-in-dashboard-design/)), headline "כל הציוד הקריטי זמין" at 64 px, subline "12 פריטים במעקב · הבדיקה האחרונה עברה בהצלחה" at 32 px, and a single row of small equipment tiles with green checks as evidence of monitoring. Alert: the stage becomes a grid of 1–4 large equipment cards (device name 40 px, location 32 px, elapsed-downtime timer 64 px, slow 2 s pulse on the card edge only). Unconfigured or stale-feed: full-stage amber takeover with explicit instructions — never the current contradiction of a success message beside an "אין ציוד קריטי מוגדר" circle. The bottom band holds three equal cards: אחראים as "0/5 סומנו" with an LTR-filling segmented bar and names at 32 px; חשמל with its three figures at 64 px and semantic color applied only to non-zero alerts; עמדות עגינה likewise, with bidi-isolated numerals. Optional second view (waiting list / preparedness detail) rotates in every 45 s with a 300 ms cross-fade, doubling as burn-in relief; outside clinic hours the board dims to a clock-and-status night mode.

What makes this "modern and professional" is not ornament but *legible intent*: a five-step type scale sized by viewing-distance math rather than web habit ([digitalsignage.com](https://digitalsignage.com/digital_signage/docs/guides/typography-viewing-distance/)), an elevation-based dark palette instead of black-on-black ([Material dark theme](https://m2.material.io/design/color/dark-theme.html)), state-driven full-screen composition instead of a permanently empty grid ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/empty-state-interface-design/)), motion reserved for meaning ([calm technology](https://www.calmtech.institute/calm-tech-principles)), and Hebrew treated as a first-class script — native typeface, isolated bidi numerals, and the Hebrew-specific LTR-progress exception ([Material 3](https://m3.material.io/foundations/layout/bidirectionality-rtl)) — rather than a mirrored afterthought. The acceptance test remains the corridor test: from the farthest working position, a 2-second glance must answer "is anything wrong, and is anything waiting for me?" — today it answers neither; after this redesign it should answer both without a single word being read.

### Summary of highest-leverage changes (ranked)
1. **Kiosk mode** — remove browser chrome; dedicated non-admin account ([Klipfolio](https://www.klipfolio.com/resources/articles/best-practices-large-screen-wallboard-tv-dashboard)).
2. **Fix the state logic** — resolve the "all available" vs "none defined" contradiction; make "unconfigured" and "stale" designed full-screen states ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/empty-state-interface-design/)).
3. **Promote the hero number** — 17 approvals at display size in the state strip; kill the gray banner.
4. **Rebuild the type scale** — 28 px absolute floor, 32 px body, 64 px headlines, 120–160 px heroes; Heebo/Assistant; no thin weights ([UI/UX Atlas](https://uiuxatlas.com/lessons/responsive/tv-and-10-foot-ui-design/); [Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography)).
5. **Elevation-gray dark theme, 7:1 text contrast, semantic-only status color with icon redundancy** ([Material](https://m2.material.io/design/color/dark-theme.html); [MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Accessibility/Guides/Understanding_WCAG/Perceivable/Use_of_color)).
6. **Freshness chip with amber staleness escalation + heartbeat** ([Smashing Magazine](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2025/09/ux-strategies-real-time-dashboards/)).
7. **Aggregate the sign-in list to 0/5 with LTR progress fill; bidi-isolate mixed Hebrew/Latin/numeric strings** ([Material 3](https://m3.material.io/foundations/layout/bidirectionality-rtl); [Apple RTL HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/right-to-left)).
8. **View rotation + night mode + layout drift for burn-in; safe-area margins** ([TuckBar](https://tuckbar.com/blog/pixel-shifting-for-oled-burn-in-prevention/); [safe zones](https://ux.detroit3d.com/platforms/tv-10-foot.html)).
